Three longer checks of the model rule (exploratory)

Exploratory. These checks were run once, on data package 1.34.0, and change no class shown on this page. Each repeats the model rule for adults (200 permutations) with one thing changed and is compared with the published run on the same package: 781 non-industrialized and 27,662 reference samples, 69 VANISH and 37 BloSSUM species, 114 VANISH and 48 BloSSUM genera. “Kept” means the taxon has the same class in the check.

  • The direction holds. In 913 comparisons of a classed taxon with its score in a check, the sign changed 3 times: Gemmiger (detection at 0.5 % relative abundance or more); Coriobacteriaceae (detection at 0.5 % relative abundance or more); UBA6398 (hunter-gatherers only as the non-industrialized group).
  • A stricter detection rule removes classes, VANISH more than BloSSUM. The species table already leaves out anything below 0.1 %, so the 0.1 % check applies to genera and families. At 0.5 %, 27 of the 69 VANISH species are no longer common enough to be tested. Another 23 are tested and lose the class; 20 of them still pass the significance and score thresholds and miss only the minimum prevalence difference of 10 points.
  • Health conditions in the reference group change little. 12,733 of the 27,662 reference samples and 47 of the 781 non-industrialized samples carry a recorded condition. Without them, 0 VANISH and 7 BloSSUM species are new.
  • Hunter-gatherers alone are a small group: 80 samples in 4 cohorts from Tanzania and Cameroon. 26 VANISH and 23 BloSSUM species are new. Of the 30 tested VANISH species that lose the class, 22 pass every threshold and fail only the leave-one-cohort-out rule, which is strict with 4 cohorts.

Limits: one profiler and one reference taxonomy; the permutation test and the leave-one-cohort-out rule are the published ones and were not recomputed by a second program for these runs. Sample selection, detection counts and a sample of model fits were. Tables: counts per check and level, every taxon in every check.
