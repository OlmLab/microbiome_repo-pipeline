2. Labels versus composition

The catalog's body site agrees with the site predicted from the genus profile for {{ "%.1f"|format(100 * M.agreement.agreement) }} % of {{ "{:,}".format(M.agreement.n_evaluated) }} labelled, QC-clean samples ({{ "%.1f"|format(100 * M.agreement.balanced_agreement) }} % averaged over sites). The classifier is a multinomial logistic regression on centred log-ratio genus abundances (the {{ "388" }} genera of the PCA). Each project's samples are scored by a model that never saw that project: five folds, split by project, with at most 150 training samples per project and site. On gut samples, a second model separates infants (exact age under 1 year) from adults (18 years or older). It agrees with exact ages for {{ "%.1f"|format(100 * M.agreement.life_agreement) }} % of {{ "{:,}".format(M.agreement.life_n_evaluated) }} samples.

A disagreement is a reason to look at a sample, not a correction. Nasal, lower-airway and oral communities overlap. Blood, tissue and eye samples are low-biomass and carry little microbial signal. Older infants already have adult-like communities. Confidently discordant samples (probability ≥ 0.9): {{ "{:,}".format(M.agreement.n_discordant_site) }} with another body site's profile, and {{ "{:,}".format(M.agreement.n_discordant_life) }} adult or older-adult gut samples with an infant-like profile. They are queued for a reading check before anything changes.
