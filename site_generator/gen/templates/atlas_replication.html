    How firm is that number?
    • A stricter test. The headline uses the test named in the plan, which is slightly too generous when projects disagree strongly. Under the Hartung-Knapp test, which is very cautious when there are few projects, {{ n(C.stricter.replicated) }} of {{ n(C.stricter.testable) }} replicate ({{ pct(C.stricter.replicated / C.stricter.testable) }}); read it as the cautious bound.
    • Person identifiers. Using only projects that say which samples come from the same person for at least 90 % of people, {{ n(C.person_id.replicated) }} of {{ n(C.person_id.testable) }} replicate ({{ pct(C.person_id.replicated / C.person_id.testable) }}); the same {{ n(C.person_id.testable) }} claims gave {{ n(C.person_id.same_claims_primary) }} in the headline analysis. The drop mixes two things: fewer projects per claim, and the removal of projects in which repeated samples of one person may have been counted as separate people.
    • One condition carries much of it. {{ n(C.largest.testable) }} of the {{ n(M.testable) }} testable claims and {{ n(C.largest.replicated) }} of the {{ n(M.replicated) }} replicated ones are about {{ C.largest.name }}. Without it, {{ n(C.without_largest.replicated) }} of {{ n(C.without_largest.testable) }} replicate ({{ pct(C.without_largest.replicated / C.without_largest.testable) }}); the mean over the {{ C.across_conditions.n }} conditions with at least {{ C.across_conditions.min_claims }} testable claims is {{ pct(C.across_conditions.rate) }}.
    • Most claims could not be tested. {{ n(M.registered - M.testable) }} of the {{ n(M.registered) }} claims had fewer than three independent projects for the condition ({{ n(M.untestable.fewer_than_3_projects_for_condition) }}) or a taxon too rare in them ({{ n(M.untestable.taxon_too_rare_in_projects) }}).
