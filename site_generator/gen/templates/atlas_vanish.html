    How to read this plot

    A taxon counts as detected in a sample when the profile assigns it any relative abundance above zero.{% for f in S.s4.floors %}{% if f.level == 'species' %} The smallest non-zero value these profiles record is {{ "%.3g"|format(f.min_relabund_pct) }} % of a sample's species profile{% endif %}{% endfor %}{% for f in S.s4.floors %}{% if f.level == 'genus' %} and {{ "%.3g"|format(f.min_relabund_pct) }} % of its genus profile{% endif %}{% endfor %}. Nothing on this page has been recomputed at a stricter detection threshold.

    The reference group is every quality-checked gut sample from a high-development-index country whose lifestyle is unrecorded or urban-industrialized — {{ "{:,}".format(S.s5.n_ref) }} samples from {{ "{:,}".format(S.s5.cohorts_ref) }} projects. Public archives are rich in case-control disease projects, so this group holds both healthy people and patients{% if S.s5.variant == 'measured' %}: {{ "{:,}".format(S.s5.n_condition) }} of those samples ({{ "%.0f"|format(S.s5.pct_condition) }} %) carry a recorded health condition other than healthy control, {{ "{:,}".format(S.s5.n_healthy) }} are recorded as healthy controls and {{ "{:,}".format(S.s5.n_no_value) }} carry no value at all{% endif %}. They are not separated here.

    What the two names do and do not mean, what the non-industrialized group pools, and what this plot cannot show are in the sections below.

    Where the terms come from

    {% if M.background.source %}Both terms are defined in {{ M.background.source }} (10.1016/j.cell.2023.05.046): BloSSUM is “Bloom or Selected in Societies of Urbanization/Modernization”, VANISH “Volatile and/or Associated Negatively with Industrialized Societies of Humans”.{% endif %} Earlier uses may exist; no published definition before this one was found.

    Both rules in plain words

    Model rule. For each taxon, a logistic model of being detected, with the group, the sequencing depth and a random effect for the project, so that one large project cannot carry the result. The score is the group term in log2 odds. The p-value comes from shuffling the group label between projects ({{ M.thresholds.B_primary or 200 }} shuffles for the primary contrasts), q is Benjamini-Hochberg, and a taxon is classed only if it survives leaving any one project out: q < {{ M.thresholds.Q or 0.05 }}, |score| ≥ {{ M.thresholds.S_min or 2 }}, a difference of at least {{ "%.0f"|format(100 * (M.thresholds.D_min or 0.1)) }} percentage points between the groups' mean per-project prevalences, and p ≤ {{ M.thresholds.P_loco or 0.01 }} in every refit.

    {% if S %}

    VANISH and BloSSUM here are names for a difference in how often a taxon is detected, not for absence.{% if S.s1.variant == 'none_absent' %} Every one of the {{ S.s1.n_vanish }} VANISH species on this page is detected in the industrialized reference group — in a median of {{ "{:,}".format(S.s1.med_samples_ref|int) }} of the {{ "{:,}".format(S.s1.n_ref) }} reference samples, and in a median of {{ S.s1.med_cohorts_ref|int }} of the {{ S.s1.cohorts_ref }} reference projects; the rarest is detected in {{ "{:,}".format(S.s1.min_samples_ref) }} of the {{ "{:,}".format(S.s1.n_ref) }} reference samples. The {{ S.s1.n_blossum }} BloSSUM species are likewise all detected in the non-industrialized group, in a median of {{ S.s1.med_samples_ni|int }} of its {{ S.s1.n_ni }} samples.{% else %} Of the {{ S.s1.n_vanish }} VANISH species on this page, {{ S.s1.n_vanish_detected_in_ref }} are detected in the industrialized reference group — in a median of {{ "{:,}".format(S.s1.med_samples_ref|int) }} of the {{ "{:,}".format(S.s1.n_ref) }} reference samples and in a median of {{ S.s1.med_cohorts_ref|int }} of the {{ S.s1.cohorts_ref }} reference projects — and {{ S.s1.n_vanish_absent_from_ref }} are detected in none of them. Of the {{ S.s1.n_blossum }} BloSSUM species, {{ S.s1.n_blossum_absent_from_ni }} are detected in no non-industrialized sample.{% endif %}

    {% endif %}

    Simple rule. No lifestyle labels at all: each person is placed by the HDI of their country (industrialized ≥ 0.80, non-industrialized < 0.70, transitional in between and not tested), a taxon counts as carried if it is detected in any of that person's samples, and the two bands are compared with Fisher's exact test. VANISH = carried by at least 20 % of non-industrialized and at most 5 % of industrialized people, q < 0.05, in at least two countries; BloSSUM is the mirror image.

    {% if S %}

    The simple rule compares people by the development index of their country with Fisher's exact test. It makes no adjustment for sequencing depth and does not account for which project a person came from; the model rule does both (a depth covariate, a project random effect, project-label permutations and a leave-one-project-out check). Where the two rules disagree, the model rule is the one to trust.

    What a “StIL candidate” is, and what it is not

    StIL — “stable independent of lifestyle” — is a third class named in {{ M.stil.citation or "Carter, Liu, Olm et al., Nature 658 (2026)" }} ({{ M.stil.doi or '10.1038/s41586-026-11106-1' }}): species that are prevalent across lifestyles and whose strains are nevertheless isolated between two distant non-industrialized populations. That paper gives no prevalence threshold and publishes no per-species list, and the strain half needs assembled genomes, which these read-based profiles do not carry. The preprint version is 10.1101/2025.08.15.670570.

    What is marked here is therefore only the prevalence half, with our own threshold, and it is called a candidate everywhere: a taxon carried by at least {{ "%.0f"|format(100 * (M.stil.min_prev or 0.2)) }} % of both groups, not VANISH and not BloSSUM, whose difference between the groups is smaller than two-fold — under the model rule the score interval (score ± 1.96 standard errors) stays inside ±{{ M.stil.max_score or 1 }} and stays there when any one project is left out; under the simple rule |lfc| < {{ M.stil.max_score or 1 }}, in at least two countries. It says that a difference of that size is excluded, not that something was discovered: with shuffled labels many common taxa would qualify, by design, and no p-value is attached.{% if M.stil and M.stil.computed_model_rule %}{% set ns = namespace(sp=0, ge=0, fa=0) %}{% for x in M.analyses %}{% if x.contrast == 'C1' and x.stratum == 'adult' %}{% if x.level == 'species' %}{% set ns.sp = x.n_stil|int %}{% elif x.level == 'genus' %}{% set ns.ge = x.n_stil|int %}{% elif x.level == 'family' %}{% set ns.fa = x.n_stil|int %}{% endif %}{% endif %}{% endfor %} Under the model rule, for adults against high-HDI countries, {{ ns.sp }} species, {{ ns.ge }} genera and {{ ns.fa }} famil{{ 'y' if ns.fa == 1 else 'ies' }} are StIL candidates. Under the model rule the class is worked out for adults only.{% endif %}{% if M.stil and not M.stil.computed_model_rule %} Under the model rule the StIL candidates are not computed in this build, because their leave-one-project-out check needs one more run of the fitting step; {{ M.stil.pending_model_rule_total or 0 }} taxa ({% for k, v in (M.stil.pending_model_rule or {}).items() %}{{ v }} {{ k }}{% if not loop.last %}, {% endif %}{% endfor %}) meet the other conditions from the stored fits and await it, and they are listed in stil_model_pending.csv without being given the class.{% endif %}

    Limits

    The non-industrialized group rests on few independent projects, and one project contributes much of it; leaving one project out at a time is the guard. Sandpiper assigns reads to GTDB genomes, so a taxon with no reference genome is invisible, and non-industrialized communities are the less well sequenced ones. About half the catalog has no profile at all. Lifestyle labels are read from project records and are incomplete; the external labels come from one paper's table. This page shows no result by health condition.

The two bands are not far apart to begin with:{% if bdI and bdN %} the median non-industrialized adult person has {{ "{:,}".format(bdN.median_root_coverage|int) }} of root coverage against {{ "{:,}".format(bdI.median_root_coverage|int) }} for the median industrialized one.{% endif %} The classes nevertheless move with depth.{% if q1 and q3 and q5 %} Averaged over the VANISH species, carriage in the non-industrialized band rises from {{ "%.0f"|format(100 * q1.mean_prev_nonind) }} % in the shallowest fifth to {{ "%.0f"|format(100 * q3.mean_prev_nonind) }} % in the middle fifth, and carriage in the industrialized band from {{ "%.1f"|format(100 * q1.mean_prev_ind) }} % in the shallowest to {{ "%.1f"|format(100 * q5.mean_prev_ind) }} % in the deepest.     Not yet done

    Three further checks that the critique invites are not done, and nothing on this page rests on them; METHOD_v2.md says what each would show.

    • A detection-threshold sweep. Recomputing every prevalence and score with detection set at a relative abundance of 0.01 % and of 0.1 %, instead of anything above zero, would show whether a class rests on marginal detections.
    • A hunter-gatherer-only contrast. Running the model rule with the non-industrialized group cut back to hunter-gatherer and indigenous-community samples would show how much of a class is the shift to agriculture rather than to industrialization.
    • A rerun without samples from a person with a recorded condition. Rebuilding the reference group from samples recorded as healthy or with no condition recorded would show whether the disease composition of the public archives moves any class.
