Two tiers, one rule: every value on this site points back to a record in a public archive or an open paper.

About this page

The registry classifies every shotgun-metagenome study reached in ENA (INSDC BioProjects from ENA, NCBI SRA and DDBJ) from its archive record — human host is one of the classified attributes ({{ reg.stats.n_human|fmt }} of {{ reg.stats.n_studies|fmt }} studies are human = yes or mixed). The catalog curates the human-gut studies of the registry, all ages, at sample level: four routes recover values, each committed only with a verbatim evidence quote (≤ 12 words), a labelled source, a locator, a route and a confidence; unknown stays unknown. Every release is immutable and versioned (Downloads & releases); code, configuration and vocabularies live in microbiome_repo-pipeline.

1 · Registry: enumeration and classification

Every INSDC BioProject with METAGENOMIC / WGS-type runs — deposited in ENA, NCBI SRA or DDBJ and enumerated through ENA's mirror (portal API) in dated slices — is a registry study; the enumeration reaches every shotgun-metagenome study, not only human ones, and each is then classified for host (registry_universe_audit.csv proves completeness per slice). Per study the questions are: host (human / non-human / mixed / unknown), body sites from a controlled vocabulary anchored to UBERON ({{ reg.n_uberon }} terms), life stages, population flags, assay (shotgun / amplicon / genome / transcriptome / other) and access. Deterministic rules decide what the archive fields settle (host taxon, library fields, numeric age with unit); the rest is classified by two independent model replicates over title, description and sample attributes, disagreements adjudicated by a stronger model; every call carries its evidence rows. {{ reg.stats.n_classified|fmt }} of {{ reg.stats.n_studies|fmt }} studies are classified ({{ reg.stats.pct_classified }} %); {{ reg.stats.n_pending|fmt }} are pending. The sample tier harvests every BioSample of the human studies and normalises site, age, sex and country from its attributes ({{ reg.stats.n_harvested|fmt }} BioSamples).

2 · Catalog scope

A registry study enters the catalog when its host is human, its assay is shotgun and gut / stool is among its body sites (Scope shows the funnel and what is left out). Every harvested BioSample of such a study is a catalog sample; its body_site_class says whether it is a gut sample (primary), has no site attribute (unknown) or belongs to another site of the same study (excluded / linked). Age categories (neonate, infant, child, adolescent, adult, elderly) come from the numeric age when known, else from the sample's stated life stage, a cohort-wide statement, or the study's primary life stage — the basis is recorded per sample.

4 · Evidence and confidence

Every row of gut_sample_determinations.parquet carries evidence_source (e.g. sample.attr.host_age, paper.supplement.<file>, paper.fulltext.methods, paper.abstract), evidence_locator (attribute key, PMCID, PMID or accession), evidence_quote (≤ 12 words, verbatim), determined_by (parser or model role and version) and a confidence tier. Confidence is an engine tier, not a calibrated probability (Fields › Confidence). Model ids are resolved per role at run time from config/models.yaml; the exact model is recorded on every model-derived row.

5 · Cohorts, papers, authors

Papers are linked to studies from Europe PMC (accession mentions, data-availability statements) and NCBI BioProject publication records, with a relation (own_data when the paper describes the deposit, unsure when it may re-analyse it). Authors are the Europe PMC author lists of those papers, string-matched and not disambiguated. Cohorts group studies that share an own-data paper or a curated cohort record (connected components); the grouping is recomputed at every release.

6 · Releases and provenance

Releases are immutable and numbered (R2026.n); each maps to a data-package semver and to Git tags in the data and site repositories. Fact tables carry release_added / release_retired / package_added; the changes pages list what each release added. The site is generated deterministically from the package (byte-identical rebuilds); data/manifest.json lists every data file with its SHA-256.

7 · Limitations

Why some values are still missing. Every project went through the sources we can reach: the archive records (all BioSample attributes; sample names, titles and library names decoded per project with two independent model replicates that must agree), the abstract and archive description, the open full text (methods prose and article tables), the open supplementary tables, and external curated resources. Projects without a linked paper were searched again by accession and title.   • Coverage is bounded by what submitters deposited and what open papers state: {{ (100 * stats.coverage_all.age_at_collection_days)|round|int }} % of samples have an age, {{ (100 * stats.coverage_all.health_condition)|round|int }} % a health-condition code, {{ (100 * stats.coverage_all.sex)|round|int }} % a sex, {{ (100 * stats.coverage_all.antibiotic_exposure)|round|int }} % an antibiotic-exposure value.
  • Case–control cohorts stay unknown at sample level unless an attribute or table names the group; cohort-wide codes are assigned only when every sequenced subject shares the condition.
  • Archive ages without a unit are read as years for studies outside the infant extension; ambiguous or conflicting values are not committed.
  • R2 reads machine-readable supplements (xlsx / xls / csv / tsv / docx tables, nested archives) that join on sample, run, alias or subject identifiers; PDF-only supplements are not parsed; papers that are not open access contribute only their abstract.
  • Values added in the deep pass fill gaps only: they never replace a value from an equal or higher-ranked route, and where both exist they agree for sex, country and age in ≥ 98 % of compared samples.
  • Registry classification of pending studies and the model-based calls carry their evidence but are not human-reviewed; use the Flag an issue link on any study or cohort page.
  • Sandpiper community profiles are shown where they exist; the registry records how many runs of each study are in the Sandpiper snapshot.
