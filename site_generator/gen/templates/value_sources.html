How to read these numbers

Facts, not columns. Related columns form one concept and count once: country, region, locality, site and latitude / longitude are one location; a coded value and its free-text detail (e.g. health condition + diagnosis text) are one value; gestational age and preterm status are one prematurity fact. A concept counts once per sample and source — a sample whose country comes from the archive and whose recruitment hospital comes from the paper has one location fact from each. Precedence. Each sample has at most one current value per column; when several sources give it, the archive record (R1) wins over supplementary tables (R2), then the paper's full text (R3), then the abstract or project description (R4), so the counts show what each source adds to the shipped table. Varies by sample is the share of facts in project × concept combinations where the source gives more than one distinct value; the rest are single statements for a whole project, such as "all participants were recruited in Kenya" or "no antibiotics in the 3 months before sampling". Values of the infant extension are counted by the same routes; 'unknown' values are not counted. ({{ extra.n_rows|fmt }} column values underlie the {{ extra.n_values|fmt }} facts.)

Longitudinal sampling and relatedness: how they are derived

Subjects. {{ dz.n_subj_real|fmt }} samples carry a subject identifier read from a source (BioSample attributes such as host_subject_id, sample names decoded by two agreeing rules, participant columns of supplementary tables). {{ dz.n_subj_gen|fmt }} more carry a generated identifier (the sample accession, marked generated on the sample page) because the paper or archive states that every sequenced stool sample comes from a different person — for example "a single stool sample was collected from each participant", or a stated number of participants equal to the number of samples with no mention of repeated sampling. Two independent model readings must agree, and it is never done for longitudinal, before–after, multi-visit, replicate or pooled designs.

Longitudinal projects ({{ dz.n_long|fmt }}, with {{ dz.n_people_rep|fmt }} people sampled more than once and {{ dz.n_samples_rep|fmt }} samples; plus {{ dz.n_rep_unres|fmt }} projects whose source describes repeated sampling but whose samples cannot yet be assigned to people). A person's samples are ordered on one time axis, chosen in this order: age at collection (when every sample has it and it varies), full collection dates, then timepoint labels converted to days ("week 4" = 28 d, "month 6" = 183 d, "baseline" and "birth" = 0). From these the project page shows the number of people sampled repeatedly, samples per person, follow-up length (first to last sample per person; median and maximum) and the median interval between consecutive samples, with a timeline of up to 36 people. The sampling schedule and time origin quoted from the paper are shown alongside{% if dz.axes %} (time axes used: {% for k, n in dz.axes.items() %}{{ k }} {{ n|fmt }}{% if not loop.last %}, {% endif %}{% endfor %} projects){% endif %}.

Relatedness and shared settings ({{ dz.n_rel|fmt }} projects; {{ dz.n_fam|fmt }} families, {{ dz.n_mc|fmt }} of them with a sampled parent and child; {{ dz.n_set|fmt }} shared settings). Four fields record it per sample: family_id (the source's family, dyad, household or twin-pair id), family_role (mother, father, parent, child, twin, sibling, partner, grandparent, other relative, household member), and shared_setting_type / shared_setting_id (a household, village, tribe, daycare or school, care home, ward, military unit, team or workplace that at least two sampled people share — never the whole cohort or the city of recruitment). They come from archive attributes, sample-name tokens whose meaning the source states, supplementary tables and paper text, each with its quote; relatedness is never inferred from similar ages or a shared country. Families are summarised as structures (e.g. "mother + child×2"), drawn as small diagrams on the project page, and listed in gut_relatedness.parquet (one row per family or shared setting); relations that roles cannot express — twin zygosity, sibling pairs, FMT donor → recipient — are in gut_relations.parquet{% if dz.rel_kinds %}.
